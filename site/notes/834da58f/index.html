Quaternions are the even subalgebra of Cl(3,0,0)

The The even subalgebra holds the rotations of Cl(3,0,0) is spanned by and has dimension four. Because the The pseudoscalar is the whole space as one blade squares to , the three bivectors behave exactly like :

So the quaternions are not a separate invention. They are the even part of the Euclidean Clifford algebra, and a unit quaternion is a rotor: for the unit bivector of the rotation plane. The familiar quaternion vector part is the bivector part, read through the dual; that is where the "axis times sine" description of a rotation comes from, and why it only works in three dimensions.

open Geom

let q = Nx.create Nx.float32 [| 4 |] [| 1.; 0.; 0.; 0. |] in
let m = Motor.of_quaternion q in
Nx.to_array (Motor.to_quaternion m)   (* [|1.; 0.; 0.; 0.|] *)
Nx.to_array (Motor.rotation m)        (* 3x3 identity, row-major *)

The library bridges the two representations explicitly, because camera and inertial code often hands you a quaternion: Motor.of_quaternion and Motor.to_quaternion. Everything else — composition, interpolation, application — is the motor API, and the double cover (Rotors compose by multiplication, twice around) is inherited unchanged.
