Wedge the points, then dualise

#ga/model

The outer product of conformal points spans the projective subspace through them, and on the null cone the blade represents the intersection of all null cones containing it. Dualising turns that span into a constraint:

  • four points a sphere: ;
  • three points and a plane: ;
  • three points a circle: ;
  • two points and a line: ;
  • two points a point pair: , already direct.

The is exactly the indicator of "flat" (Round versus flat: the e∞ contraction decides): add it for a plane or a line, omit it for a sphere or a circle.

open Geom
let p = Cga.point3 in
let circle = Cga.circle (p 1. 0. 0.) (p 0. 1. 0.) (p (-1.) 0. 0.) in
let line   = Cga.line (p 1. 0. 1.) (p (-1.) 0. 1.) in
let plane  = Cga.plane_through (p 1. 0. 0.) (p 0. 1. 0.) (p 0. 0. 1.) in
let sphere = Cga.sphere_through (p 1. 0. 0.) (p (-1.) 0. 0.)
                                (p 0. 1. 0.) (p 0. 0. 1.) in
Nx.item [] (Cga.circle_radius2 circle)   (* 1. *)

This is the cleanest way to build objects, and the reason the library's constructors are mostly "wedge the points, then dualise". It also means you never have to solve for a sphere; the algebra does the linear algebra, and the projective scale is the only thing left to normalise.
