A point pair is a hyperbolic 2-blade

#ga/model

So a point pair of distinct real points is a hyperbolic blade with a positive square, and it is null exactly when the points coincide. It is a zero-dimensional object: not a constraint blade, and best stored in its direct form rather than dualised.

open Geom

let x = Cga.point3 0. 0. 0. and y = Cga.point3 2. 0. 0. in
let p = Cga.point_pair x y in
Nx.item [] (Cga.coeff (Cga.gp p p) (Blade.of_int 0))   (* 4. = d^4/4 *)
Nx.item [] (Cga.incident x p)                          (* true *)
Nx.item [] (Cga.incident (Cga.point3 1. 0. 0.) p)      (* false *)

The scalar is the invariant that survives an arbitrary rescaling of either point, which is why it is the right thing to test rather than the raw coefficients. Point pairs appear as tangential intersections: when a line touches a sphere, the meet is a double point pair, and its vanishing square is the tangency condition. The library also obtains them as the dual of when a line and a sphere intersect (A line is a 2-blade).
