Distance is an inner product

#ga/model

For two conformal points and ,

That is the one identity to remember. Everything else in the conformal model — incidence, angle, intersection, even the round/flat distinction — is a corollary of it or of the same contraction pattern.

open Geom

let x = Cga.point3 1. 1. 0. and y = Cga.point3 4. 5. 0. in
Nx.item [] (Cga.scalar_prod x y)   (* -12.5 *)
Nx.item [] (Cga.distance2 x y)     (* 25. *)
Nx.item [] (Cga.distance x y)      (* 5. *)

The minus sign is not cosmetic: it makes the scalar product of distinct real points negative definite in the difference, so a zero scalar product means coincident points and the squared distance is with no absolute value. The library's distance2 is exactly -2 * the scalar product, and distance guards the square root against the small negative values that rounding produces for coincident points.

Two consequences worth carrying: incidence is the same contraction against a different blade (Round versus flat: the e∞ contraction decides), and the whole object layer is arranged so that a point and an object are tested with one expression (The working representation stores the lower grade). That uniformity is why predicates become polynomial and why the conformal model is pleasant to differentiate through (Write task objectives as conformal residuals).
