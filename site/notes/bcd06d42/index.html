Rotating about an axis through a point

#ga/motion

A rotor about the origin, and a translator by the position vector of a point , combine into a rotation about the axis through :

That conjugation is the general pattern for turning an origin-centred motion into a motion about another frame, and it is the same formula as a change of basis in linear algebra. The result is a motor with both rotational and translational parts.

open Geom
let v3 x y z = Nx.create Nx.float32 [| 3 |] [| x; y; z |] in
let sc x = Nx.scalar Nx.float32 x in

let about_point ~c ~axis ~angle =
  let t = Motor.translate c in
  let r = Motor.rotate ~axis ~angle in
  Motor.mul t (Motor.mul r (Motor.inverse t))

let m = about_point ~c:(v3 1. 0. 0.) ~axis:(v3 0. 0. 1.)
                  ~angle:(sc (Float.pi /. 2.)) in
Nx.to_array (Cga.coords (Cga.apply_motor m (Cga.point3 2. 0. 0.)))
(* [|1.; 1.; 0.|]: the point swings round the centre at (1,0,0) *)

In a kinematic chain the same idea appears as the fixed transform between link frames, and each joint is either a rotor about its own local axis or a translator along it (Forward kinematics is a motor product). A screw joint is the general case: the exponential of a twist that has both parts (The SE(3) exponential turns a twist into a screw).

Note that the order of the product is the matrix order (Motors compose like matrices), and that conjugation preserves the versor structure, so the result is a motor of the same norm; no renormalisation is needed for a single composition, though long products should call Motor.normalize (Numerical cautions in a projective algebra).
