ScLERP interpolates along the group exponential

#ga/motion

Because motors form a Lie group, interpolation is the group exponential:

This is screw linear interpolation: the motion follows the screw that takes to , at constant twist. The endpoints are exact up to rounding. It is the motor analogue of quaternion slerp, and the reason exp and log are in the public interface rather than buried in the kinematics layer.

open Geom
let v3 x y z = Nx.create Nx.float32 [| 3 |] [| x; y; z |] in
let sc x = Nx.scalar Nx.float32 x in

let m0 = Motor.one in
let m1 =
  Motor.mul (Motor.translate3 1.3 0. 0.55)
    (Motor.rotate ~axis:(v3 0. 0. 1.) ~angle:(sc (Float.pi *. 0.85)))
in
let at t =
  Cga.coords (Cga.apply_motor (Motor.slerp m0 m1 (sc t)) (Cga.point3 0. 0. 0.))
in
Nx.to_array (at 0.5)   (* [|0.65; -0.5124; 0.275|] *)

Compared with interpolating the eight coefficients independently, ScLERP has two properties that matter: the intermediate values are exact rigid motions, and the path is independent of the frame the two endpoints were expressed in. Compared with a straight line in translation plus a slerp in rotation, it is the same thing only when the screw axis passes through the origin.

alpha is a tensor, so a batch of interpolants is one call (The last axis is the blade axis). The endpoints and must be normalised motors, or the logarithm absorbs the scale instead of the motion (Numerical cautions in a projective algebra).
