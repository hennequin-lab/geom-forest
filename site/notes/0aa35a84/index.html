Exponentiating a simple bivector closes in three cases

#ga/foundation

A bivector generates a one-parameter group of versors through . When is simple — , equivalently is a scalar — the series terminates in a closed form:

The three cases are the three geometries a single plane can carry. A negative square is an elliptic rotation: that is a rotor. A positive square is a hyperbolic boost: a translation in the conformal model (A translator is a null rotation) or a dilation (A dilation is a boost in the e4e5 plane). A zero square is a null rotation: a translation again, or a transversion (A transversion bends space without moving infinity), or a shear.

open Geom

module V3 = Versor.Make (E3)

let b = E3.gp (E3.op E3.e1 E3.e2) (E3.scalar (-.Float.pi /. 4.)) in
let r = V3.exp_simple b in                 (* a 45 degree rotor *)
Nx.to_array (E3.to_vector (E3.apply_rotor r E3.e1))

A general bivector in five dimensions is a sum of at most two commuting simple bivectors, so the same closed form applies after a spectral decomposition. The library implements exp_simple today and stages the general exp/log behind the Versor interface; the robotics path instead uses the packed Motor.exp, which is the same map on a smaller algebra (One motor algebra, two models).
