Invert a blade with its reverse

#ga/foundation

For a blade , the product is a scalar, so inverting is a division:

A blade with is null and has no inverse at all; a blade with is invertible and is called non-null. Null blades are not pathological in this forest — the degenerate direction is null, conformal points are null vectors, and translators are generated by a null bivector — they simply cannot be normalised metrically.

open Geom

let b = E3.op (E3.vector3 2. 0. 0.) (E3.vector3 0. 3. 0.) in
Nx.item [] (E3.norm2 b)                       (* 36. *)
E3.to_array (E3.inverse b)                    (* -e12 / 6 *)
E3.to_array (E3.sandwich b b)                 (* b *)

inverse implements that formula and does not detect singularity, so check norm2 first when an object may be null. Because inverting is a reverse, the three involutions do most of the sign bookkeeping in the library. normalize is the metric normalisation ; the projective normalisations of spheres, planes and points are different operations with different jobs, and mixing them up is the most common source of silently wrong geometry.
