It is a grade-1 vector, not a four-blade. Its square is the squared radius, , and it satisfies , so it is a round object (Round versus flat: the e∞ contraction decides). Incidence is the same contraction as always: a point is on the sphere exactly when , and the value of that contraction is the signed offset from the surface,

open Geom
let v3 x y z = Nx.create Nx.float32 [| 3 |] [| x; y; z |] in
let sc x = Nx.scalar Nx.float32 x in

let s = Cga.sphere ~center:(v3 0. 0. 0.) ~radius:(sc 2.) in
Nx.item [] (Cga.sphere_radius2 s)                     (* 4. *)
Nx.item [] (Cga.incident (Cga.point3 2. 0. 0.) s)     (* true *)
Nx.to_array (Cga.coords (Cga.sphere_center s))        (* [|0.; 0.; 0.|] *)

Three practical notes. The representation is homogeneous, so Cga.normalize_sphere divides by before comparing radii. And the four-blade representative is the wedge of four points on the sphere (Four points determine a sphere), which is the dual picture; the vector is the working representation (The working representation stores the lower grade).
