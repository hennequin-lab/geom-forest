The SE(3) exponential turns a twist into a screw

#ga/motion

A general element of the rigid Lie algebra is a twist , with angular velocity and linear velocity . Its exponential is a screw motion. In matrix form, with the skew matrix of , and

one has , and . The implementation uses this closed form with small-angle limits at zero (Numerical cautions in a projective algebra).

open Geom

let twist = Nx.create Nx.float32 [| 6 |]
              [| 0.; 0.; Float.pi /. 2.; 0.; 0.; 1. |] in
let m = Motor.exp twist in
Nx.to_array (Motor.log m)      (* [|0.; 0.; 1.571; 0.; 0.; 1.|] *)
Nx.to_array (Cga.coords (Cga.apply_motor m (Cga.point3 1. 0. 0.)))
(* [|0.; 1.; 1.|]: a quarter turn about z, then a unit lift *)

The exponential is the bridge between the two languages robotics uses. In the Lie-algebra language, a twist is a velocity and the exponential integrates it over one unit of time. In the algebra language, the twist is a bivector and the exponential is the bivector exponential. The library exposes the dense version too, and the tests check that the two agree — but the packed Motor.exp is what kinematics should call, because it works in eight coefficients rather than 32.

The screw interpretation is worth keeping: any rigid motion is a rotation about some axis in space, combined with a translation along that same axis. The exponential makes that decomposition algorithmic, and the logarithm (The logarithm recovers the twist) recovers it.
