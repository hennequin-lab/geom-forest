Cl(4,1) is the conformal model of 3D space

#ga/model

The conformal model takes five orthonormal directions — four positive, one negative — and defines two null combinations

with and . The Euclidean directions are untouched. This is Cl(4,1), 32 coefficients, and its objects are a strict superset of everything in the Euclidean and projective models.

The extra dimensions buy four things at once: Euclidean points become null vectors, distance becomes an inner product, spheres and planes become vectors (A sphere is a vector, A plane is a vector), and the whole Möbius group — rotations, translations, dilations and inversions — becomes the group of versors (The conformal group has ten generators).

open Geom

(Signature.to_string Cga.signature, Cga.size)   (* ("(4,1,0)", 32) *)
Cga.to_array Cga.einf                           (* 1 on e4 and e5 *)
Cga.to_array Cga.e0                             (* 1/2 on e5, -1/2 on e4 *)
Nx.item [] (Cga.scalar_prod Cga.e0 Cga.einf)    (* -1. *)

The storage is in the orthonormal basis, not the null basis, for a reason that matters for the software (Store the orthonormal basis, read the null basis). The object layer stores the lower of the two dual representatives (The working representation stores the lower grade), which is what makes incidence a single contraction. Rigid motions are handled by the packed eight-component A motor is a dual quaternion so that kinematics never pays for 32 coefficients.
