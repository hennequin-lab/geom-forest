Ideal points carry weight zero

#ga/model

A projective point is a trivector, and the coefficient of the Euclidean pseudoscalar is its weight. Write for that coefficient. A finite point can be rescaled so that ; that is the affine slice, and it is what Pga.normalize_point and Pga.point_coords use.

A point of weight zero is not a location at all. It is a direction: the point at infinity in that direction. Algebraically it is a trivector whose coefficient vanishes,

and it is obtained with Pga.ideal_point bold(d), where is the direction vector. Directions and locations live in the same algebra, distinguished by one coefficient.

open Geom
let v3 x y z = Nx.create Nx.float32 [| 3 |] [| x; y; z |] in

let i = Pga.ideal_point (v3 0. 0. 1.) in
Nx.item [] (Pga.weight i)         (* 0. *)
Nx.item [] (Pga.is_ideal i)       (* true *)
Nx.item [] (Pga.is_ideal (Pga.point3 0. 0. 1.))  (* false *)

This is the mechanism behind the object table: a line through two finite points has a direction and a moment (Meet and join in PGA), and the direction part is the ideal point it contains.
