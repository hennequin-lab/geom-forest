A multivector is a coefficient vector over blades

#ga/foundation

A multivector is an element of the algebra: a formal sum

where is the dimension of the algebra, the sum runs over the index sets , is the basis blade of that index set, and is its real coefficient. In software it is just the array of those coefficients, in increasing bitmask order. Nothing about the algebra is hidden in the type; the operations read and write that array.

open Geom

let a = E3.gp E3.e1 E3.e2 in
E3.to_array a                 (* [|0.; 0.; 0.; 1.; 0.; 0.; 0.; 0.|] *)
Blade.to_string (Blade.of_int 3)  (* "e12": index 3 has the coefficient 1 *)
E3.coeff a (Blade.of_int 3)   (* 1. *)

coeff and with_coeff are the accessors that name a blade directly, and they are the API you want in tests and in debugging. grade k keeps one grade and zeros the rest; even and odd do it for a whole parity.

The coefficient vector is also the batching axis convention. A single multivector has shape [|2^n|], a batch of of them has shape [|b; 2^n|], and an image of points has shape [|h; w; 2^n|]. The leading axes are ordinary broadcast axes for Nx, so every algebra operation is one batched contraction over the last axis. The last axis is the blade axis spells out the consequences; Store the orthonormal basis, read the null basis explains why the coefficient basis is the orthonormal one even in the conformal model.
