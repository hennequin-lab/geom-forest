Inverse kinematics is a log-and-step loop

#ga/motion

A local inverse-kinematics step is a Gauss–Newton iteration on a pose error. In motors, the error is a twist computed by the logarithm (The logarithm recovers the twist), and the update is applied by the exponential (The SE(3) exponential turns a twist into a screw):

Here is the desired pose, the current pose, the six-vector pose error, the vector of joint coordinates, the pseudo-inverse of the task Jacobian, and the joint update. The error is expressed in the world frame, because the left factor acts on the left. For a tool-frame error, reverse the order: .

The Jacobian columns are the joint twists expressed in the same frame as the error, either analytic or obtained by differentiating the chain (Forward kinematics is a motor product) with Rune.

open Geom

(* the pose error as a twist, in the world frame *)
let error_twist ~target ~current =
  Motor.log (Motor.mul target (Motor.inverse current))   (* shape [|6|] *)

(* move a joint twist into the tool frame *)
let to_tool_frame m xi =
  Motor.log (Motor.mul (Motor.mul m (Motor.exp xi)) (Motor.inverse m))

The essential structure is four calls: Motor.inverse for the current pose, Motor.log for the error, Motor.adjoint to express the error in the right frame (The adjoint turns a sandwich into a matrix), and Motor.exp for the update. Everything else is linear algebra, and it is ordinary Nx, so the same loop runs on CPU or GPU and batches over several targets at once (The last axis is the blade axis).

The two classic failure modes survive: local minima (the log branch is only valid near the identity) and joint limits (a constraint on , not on the algebra). Neither is a defect of the representation; both are why the step is usually damped.
