Reverse and the magnitude of a blade

#ga/foundation

Reverse turns every product around: . On a grade- blade it is a sign,

so scalars and vectors are unchanged, bivectors and trivectors flip sign, and four-blades are unchanged again. The sign pattern is the one that makes a blade's magnitude come out positive:

open Geom

E3.to_array (E3.reverse (E3.op E3.e1 E3.e2))   (* -e12 *)
Nx.item [] (E3.norm2 (E3.op E3.e1 E3.e2))      (* 1. *)
Nx.item [] (E3.norm (E3.op (E3.vector3 2. 0. 0.)
                            (E3.vector3 0. 3. 0.)))      (* 6. *)

For a blade, is always a scalar, which is why the inverse of a blade is just divided by that number. For a general versor, the reverse is the inverse up to a sign: unit versors satisfy . That is what makes the sandwich cheap: the inverse is a reverse, no linear solve.

Reverse is one of three standard involutions — the only one that is an anti-automorphism rather than an automorphism.
