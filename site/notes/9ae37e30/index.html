Basis blades are bitmasks

#ga/foundation

A blade is an ordered product of distinct basis vectors. Number the basis vectors from to and encode a blade by the integer whose bit is set exactly when occurs in it. Then:

  • the grade is the population count of the integer;
  • the product index is the bitwise exclusive or, because a repeated letter cancels: ;
  • the sign is the parity of the permutation that sorts the concatenation of and ;
  • the metric factor is the product of over the intersection , and is zero when a null direction appears in both factors.

This is the whole multiplication algorithm. For there are blades and ordered pairs, so the table is 4 KiB of integers, computed once. Blade owns the index arithmetic and knows nothing about the metric, which is why it can be shared by all the models.

open Geom

let b = Blade.of_int 3 in
Blade.to_string b                     (* "e12" *)
Blade.grade b                         (* 2 *)
Blade.mul_sign b b                    (* -1: e12 e12 = -1 *)
Blade.to_string (Blade.complement ~n:3 b)  (* "e3" *)
Blade.complement_sign ~n:3 b          (* 1: e12 and e3 = e123 *)

Blade.all n returns the blades in increasing bitmask order, which is exactly the order of the coefficient array of a multivector. The bitmask encoding is also why the library's storage choice in Store the orthonormal basis, read the null basis matters: the xor rule needs orthogonality.
