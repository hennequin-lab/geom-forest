In PGA, incidence is the wedge

#ga/model

A point lies on a plane exactly when their wedge vanishes:

That is the plane equation read as a statement about blades: the point is inside the subspace the plane represents. Nothing is contracted, nothing is normalised, and no metric is used. It works because the point is the intersection of three planes: if one of them is , the wedge is dependent.

open Geom

let pi = Pga.plane3 0. 0. 1. 0. in
Nx.item [] (Pga.point_on_plane (Pga.point3 1. 1. 0.) pi)  (* true *)
Nx.item [] (Pga.point_on_plane (Pga.point3 1. 1. 2.) pi)  (* false *)
let l = Pga.line_through (Pga.point3 0. 0. 0.) (Pga.point3 0. 0. 1.) in
Nx.item [] (Pga.point_on_line (Pga.point3 0. 0. 0.5) l)   (* true *)

Two subtleties. First, the plain wedge of a point (grade 3) and a line (grade 2) is always zero in four dimensions, so point_on_line uses the complement dual instead — the library hides that behind the predicate. Second, in a degenerate metric the wedge does not reduce to a scalar contraction of a dual point with a plane, the way it does in the conformal model (Round versus flat: the e∞ contraction decides). Incidence here is genuinely exterior, and Meet and join in PGA builds the intersection calculus on top of it.
