Four points determine a sphere

#ga/model

The outer product of four conformal points is a grade-4 blade, and its dual is the sphere vector through them:

This is the conformal version of a linear solve: the wedge is the determinant, and the dual reads the answer off the coefficients. No normalisation is needed until you want the radius.

open Geom
let p = Cga.point3 in
let s = Cga.sphere_through (p 1. 0. 0.) (p (-1.) 0. 0.)
                           (p 0. 1. 0.) (p 0. 0. 1.) in
Nx.item [] (Cga.sphere_radius2 s)   (* 1. *)
Nx.item [] (Cga.incident (p 0. 1. 0.) s)  (* true *)

The four points must be affinely independent: four coplanar points make the wedge vanish, and the library returns the zero blade rather than a diagnostic (Numerical cautions in a projective algebra). If you have more than four points on an approximate sphere, the right move is a least-squares fit whose residuals are the contractions — which is exactly the residual pattern, and the same one used for fitting planes and circles.

The sphere is the round counterpart of the plane through three points (Wedge the points, then dualise); both are "wedge, add for flat, dualise".
