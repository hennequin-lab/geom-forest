open Geom

let m =
  Motor.mul (Motor.translate3 0. 0. 1.)
    (Motor.rotate ~axis:(Nx.create Nx.float32 [|3|] [|0.;0.;1.|])
       ~angle:(Nx.scalar Nx.float32 (Float.pi /. 2.)))
in
Nx.to_array (Motor.log m)   (* the twist, shape [|6|] *)

The logarithm is where most robotics code touches Lie theory: a pose error, a velocity command, and an interpolation are all logarithms or exponentials. In this forest it is the workhorse behind screw interpolation and inverse kinematics.

Two cautions. The logarithm is multivalued globally — a rotation by and by are the same motor but different twists — so "the" logarithm always means the branch near the identity. And for a motor with no rotation part the formula degenerates to the pure translation , which the small-angle branch handles. For the dense algebra the same map is Versor.log, currently limited to a single simple bivector (Exponentiating a simple bivector closes in three cases).
