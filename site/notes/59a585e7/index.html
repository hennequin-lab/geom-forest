#ga/foundation

A versor is a product of invertible vectors,

Versors are the elements that act on the algebra by conjugation, and their inverses are reverses up to scale: for a versor , the product is a scalar and . After normalisation, , so the inverse is a reverse and applying a versor costs two products, not a linear solve.

The versors of a given signature form a group, and its identity component is the spin group: double-covers rotations, double-covers the Möbius group of (The conformal group has ten generators). Even versors are even, and they are called rotors when they are Euclidean rotations, motors when they are rigid motions (A motor is a dual quaternion), translators when they are translations (A translator is a null rotation).

The exponential of a bivector is the standard way to manufacture a versor (Exponentiating a simple bivector closes in three cases), and the logarithm inverts it (The logarithm recovers the twist). Versor.Make is the functor that provides exp, log, exp_simple and slerp for any algebra.
