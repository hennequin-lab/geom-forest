Cl(3,0,0) is the Euclidean rotation algebra

#ga/model

Cl(3,0,0) has three orthonormal basis vectors with and no degenerate or negative direction. Its eight coefficients split as one scalar, three vectors, three bivectors and one trivector:

grade blades interpretation
0 1 scalars
1 e1, e2, e3 Euclidean vectors
2 e12, e13, e23 rotation planes
3 e123 the pseudoscalar

The bivectors are the rotation planes, the vectors are ordinary Euclidean vectors, and the trivector is the The pseudoscalar is the whole space as one blade with . The even part is four-dimensional, and it is exactly the Quaternions are the even subalgebra of Cl(3,0,0).

Because the metric is positive definite, every non-zero blade is invertible (Invert a blade with its reverse), the The Hodge dual trades a blade for its complement is available, and there are no lightlike directions to trip over. That makes Cl(3,0,0) the clean testbed: if an identity fails here, it is your algebra that is wrong, not your convention.

open Geom

let x = E3.vector3 1. 2. 3. in
Nx.to_array (E3.to_vector x)                    (* [|1.; 2.; 3.|] *)
let b = E3.op E3.e1 E3.e2 in                    (* the e12 rotation plane *)
Nx.item [] (E3.norm2 b)                         (* 1.: unit magnitude *)
Nx.item [] (E3.grade 0 (E3.gp E3.pseudoscalar E3.pseudoscalar))  (* -1. *)

In the library E3 is the algebra used for orientation-only problems and for rotors. It is also the place to develop intuition before the projective and conformal layers add dimensions, because every object you can draw in Cl(3,0,0) is a vector, a plane or a volume; see A blade is a subspace with a magnitude.
