The regressive product is the dual of the wedge

#ga/foundation

The regressive product (or meet) of multivectors and is the outer product conjugated by the In a degenerate algebra the dual becomes the complement:

where is the signed index complement of . It lowers grade instead of raising it. Where the wedge spans the smallest subspace containing both operands, the regressive product intersects them: two planes give their line, a line and a plane give their point. This is the operation that makes projective geometry a calculus rather than a collection of formulas.

open Geom

let pi1 = Pga.plane3 1. 0. 0. 0.    (* x = 0 *)
and pi2 = Pga.plane3 0. 1. 0. 0. in (* y = 0 *)
let l = Pga.meet pi1 pi2 in
Nx.to_array (Pga.line_direction l)          (* parallel to z *)
let joined = Pga.join (Pga.point3 0. 0. 2.) (Pga.point3 1. 0. 2.) in
Nx.to_array (Pga.line_direction joined)     (* (1,0,0) up to scale *)

In Cl(3,0,1) the regressive product is implemented exactly (Algebra.regressive, exposed as Pga.join) and it is the join of points, while the wedge is the meet of planes (Meet and join in PGA). The naming flips because the model stores planes as vectors and points as trivectors. In Cl(4,1) the star exists and the regressive product is available too, but the conformal object layer prefers named constructors (Wedge the points, then dualise), because "wedge-then-dualise" is easier to audit than a universal meet that silently accepts the wrong grades.
