The even subalgebra holds the rotations

#ga/foundation

The even-grade elements of an -dimensional Clifford algebra close under multiplication, so they form a subalgebra of dimension : half of the basis blades have an even number of factors. It contains the scalars and the bivectors, hence the exponentials of bivectors, hence all rotors: every rotation is an even versor.

Two instances are worth memorising:

  • the even subalgebra of Cl(3,0,0) is Cl(0,2,0), the quaternions;
  • the even subalgebra of Cl(3,0,1) is the eight-dimensional rigid-motion algebra, the dual quaternions. 
open Geom

let g = E3.gp E3.e1 E3.e2 in
Blade.to_string (Blade.of_int 3)               (* "e12" *)
E3.to_array (E3.odd g)                         (* zero: even *)
let r = E3.rotor ~axis:(Nx.create Nx.float32 [|3|] [|0.;0.;1.|])
                 ~angle:(Nx.scalar Nx.float32 0.5) in
E3.to_array (E3.odd (E3.gp r E3.e1))           (* all of it: odd *)

The odd part is a module over the even part: multiplying an even element by an odd one stays odd. That is why a rotor can rotate a vector (odd to odd) while being even itself, and why reversing a sandwich does not change the parity of the object.

The grade split also suggests the first software optimisation: if every value you need is even, you only need half the coefficients. The library does that explicitly for motors, storing 8 components instead of 16 or 32 (One motor algebra, two models).
