Numerical cautions in a projective algebra

#ga/computing

Four failure modes account for most wrong answers. They are worth checking in this order, because each one can mask the next.

Scale. Every object is homogeneous: and are the same sphere. Fix the scale before comparing anything, with the normalisation that belongs to the object, not the metric one:

object call what it fixes
conformal point Cga.normalize_point e∞ . X = -1
conformal sphere Cga.normalize_sphere e∞ . S = -1
conformal plane Cga.normalize_plane pi^2 = 1
PGA point Pga.normalize_point weight 1
PGA plane Pga.normalize_plane unit normal
blade, versor Algebra.normalize metric magnitude 1
motor Motor.normalize M ~M = 1

Signs of squares. An imaginary sphere has , while a point pair of two real points has a positive square (A point pair is a hyperbolic 2-blade). Neither sign should be forced positive before a square root; sphere_radius2 returns the signed value on purpose.

Dual versus complement. The The Hodge dual trades a blade for its complement exists only for a non-degenerate signature; the In a degenerate algebra the dual becomes the complement exists always (PGA has no Hodge dual, and that is the point). A generic dual routed through both models silently mirrors geometry.

Degeneracy. Four coplanar points have a zero wedge, so Cga.sphere_through returns zero rather than a sphere (Four points determine a sphere). Three collinear points do not vanish: they give a flat 2-blade, the line through them, so circle_radius2 is undefined rather than zero. Check the magnitude of a constructed object, and its flat/round flag, before using it.

open Geom
let s = Cga.sphere ~center:(Nx.create Nx.float32 [|3|] [|0.;0.;0.|])
                   ~radius:(Nx.scalar Nx.float32 2.) in
Cga.to_array (Cga.normalize_sphere s)   (* scale fixed *)

Finally, float32 drift: long motor products should call Motor.normalize, and distance guards the square root of a squared distance that rounding may leave slightly negative for coincident points.
