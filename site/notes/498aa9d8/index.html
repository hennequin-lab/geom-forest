PGA objects live at fixed grades

#ga/model

In Cl(3,0,1) the object type is the grade, and the grade is the codimension:

grade blade object
1 e1, e0 plane
2 e12, e10 line
3 e123, e120 point

A plane is a vector , so it is a linear functional on points: the plane equation is the contraction. A point is the wedge of three planes, so it is a trivector, and its coefficient on is its weight (Ideal points carry weight zero). A line is the wedge of two planes, a bivector with the six Plücker components.

open Geom

let p = Pga.point3 1. 2. 3. in
Nx.item [] (Pga.weight p)             (* 1. *)
Nx.to_array (Pga.point_coords p)      (* [|1.; 2.; 3.|] *)
let l = Pga.line_through p (Pga.point3 1. 2. 4.) in
Nx.to_array (Pga.line_direction l)    (* parallel to z *)
Nx.to_array (Pga.line_moment l)       (* the moment about the origin *)

The point of the grade table is that incidence becomes a wedge, with no metric involved; In PGA, incidence is the wedge shows the predicates, and meet and join turn them into an intersection calculus.

Two normalisations are easy to confuse. Algebra.normalize is the metric one and is useless for a null object such as an ideal point. Pga.normalize_plane divides by the normal's length so that the offset is a signed distance, and Pga.normalize_point divides by the weight so that the point is on the affine slice. The numerical note explains why mixing them silently changes the geometry.
