Meet and join in PGA

#ga/model

Two operations carry the whole projective calculus:

  • meet is the outer product: two planes meet in a line, a line and a plane meet in a point. It lowers the codimension, so it raises the grade.
  • join is the regressive product: two points span a line, a point and a line span a plane.

Both are exact, polynomial, and free of division. That is the practical difference from homogeneous matrices, where intersection requires a cross product and a case analysis.

open Geom

let pi1 = Pga.plane3 1. 0. 0. 0.    (* x = 0 *)
and pi2 = Pga.plane3 0. 1. 0. 0. in (* y = 0 *)
let l = Pga.meet pi1 pi2 in
Nx.to_array (Pga.line_direction l)          (* parallel to z *)
let corner = Pga.meet l (Pga.plane3 0. 0. 1. 2.) in
Nx.to_array (Pga.point_coords corner)       (* [|0.; 0.; 2.|] *)
let same = Pga.join (Pga.point3 0. 0. 0.) (Pga.point3 0. 0. 1.) in
Nx.to_array (Pga.line_direction same)       (* parallel to z *)

The naming is worth fixing in your head: in PGA, points are trivectors and planes are vectors, so the wedge meets and the regressive product joins. In PGA, incidence is the wedge is the predicate-level version of the same story. Reading Pga.meet as "the intersection" and Pga.join as "the span" keeps the geometry straight even when the grade arithmetic feels inverted. The conformal model has the opposite convention (The working representation stores the lower grade).
