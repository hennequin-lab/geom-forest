The scalar product reads off a single number

#ga/foundation

Write for the grade-zero (scalar) part of a multivector , and for its reverse. The scalar product of two multivectors and is the single number

It is symmetric in every signature, because reversing the product gives . For vectors it coincides with the The left contraction is the metric projection: . The library exposes it as a function returning a tensor of the batch shape, because that is usually what you want to compare against zero.

open Geom

let u = E3.vector3 1. 0. 0.
and v = E3.vector3 (1. /. Float.sqrt 2.) (1. /. Float.sqrt 2.) 0. in
Nx.item [] (E3.scalar_prod u v)   (* 0.7071 *)

The reason a library needs both a contraction and a scalar product is that they differ on mixed grades and on blades in non-Euclidean signatures. The contraction returns a multivector; the scalar product throws away everything but the scalar. Incidence tests use the contraction because the sign of the result is a signed distance (Distance is an inner product), while normalisation uses , which is the squared magnitude of a blade up to the sign convention of the reverse (Reverse and the magnitude of a blade, Invert a blade with its reverse). The library's norm2 is exactly that expression.

In the conformal model the scalar product of two points is the single most useful number in the model: times their squared distance (Distance is an inner product). That is why the library's norm2 and scalar_prod are the functions you reach for when you need a distance and do not want to write one.
