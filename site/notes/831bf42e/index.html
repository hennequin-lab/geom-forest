Perpendicular directions anticommute

#ga/foundation

For orthogonal basis vectors with , the Clifford relation gives

The product of two perpendicular directions is a bivector, and swapping the order flips its sign. That sign is geometry: it is the orientation of the plane.

open Geom

let e12 = E3.gp E3.e1 E3.e2 in
let e21 = E3.gp E3.e2 E3.e1 in
Nx.item [] (E3.scalar_prod e12 e21)   (* -1. *)

So a bivector is an ordered pair of directions. and are the same plane with opposite orientations, and a rotation by in that plane is a rotation by in the other. This is why rotors have a sign ambiguity that no normalisation can remove (Rotors compose by multiplication, twice around): a full turn of the sandwich brings you back to , where is the rotor.

Non-commutativity is a feature. The The commutator makes bivectors a Lie algebra measures the failure to commute, and for bivectors it is the Lie bracket of infinitesimal motions. Rotations in different planes do not commute, and the commutator tells you exactly how they fail — which is the algebra behind the SE(3) exponential and behind every kinematic Jacobian.

What does commute is scaled copies of a single blade: a bivector commutes with itself, so the exponential is just a function of one bivector (Exponentiating a simple bivector closes in three cases).
