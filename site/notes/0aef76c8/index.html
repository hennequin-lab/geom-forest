Why geometric algebra: one product instead of a pile

#ga/foundation

Classical engineering geometry is a pile of unrelated operations. The dot product measures alignment. The cross product measures perpendicularity, but only in three dimensions, and it hands back a normal vector where the honest answer is a plane. Quaternions rotate. Homogeneous matrices translate. Plücker coordinates describe lines, in a formalism with a constraint nobody remembers. Each tool has its own composition rule, its own interpolation, its own singularities, and its own trick for differentiating through it. Using them together means remembering which conversion applies at each boundary.

Geometric algebra replaces the pile with one associative product on one vector space. The product is written by juxtaposition: is the product of the vectors and . It splits into two parts,

where is a scalar — the ordinary dot product, which measures how much the vectors agree — and is a bivector, written with the wedge symbol, which measures how much they disagree and records the plane they span. The dot product is symmetric; the wedge is antisymmetric. The single product contains both, which is why it can replace both.

Every classical operation is now a projection of that product, or a conjugation by a product of vectors:

  • the geometric product itself is the whole thing;
  • the wedge keeps the top-grade part, and describes spans and incidence;
  • the The left contraction is the metric projection keeps the metric part, and describes projections and distances;
  • the scalar product keeps only the scalar;
  • rotations and rigid motions are sandwiches by even elements.

A plane becomes a first-class object (the bivector) rather than the shadow of a normal, so the same formula works in two, three and five dimensions. That is the content of the grade story.
