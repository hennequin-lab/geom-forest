The camera projects by a PGA meet

#ga/viz

The camera is not a 4×4 matrix. It is a set of projective objects:

  • Camera.view c is the motor carrying world points into camera coordinates;
  • Camera.eye_point c is the world-space eye, as a Pga.t point;
  • Camera.image_plane c is the world-space image plane , as a Pga.t plane;
  • a view ray is the Pga line through the eye and the page point, and the projection of a world point is the meet of that ray with the image plane.

The meet performs the perspective division: the coordinates of the resulting point are the image coordinates. That is the whole projection.

open Geom
open Geom_viz

let c = Camera.create ~eye:(Primitive.p3 4. (-4.) 3.)
          ~target:(Primitive.p3 0. 0. 0.5) ~up:(Primitive.p3 0. 0. 1.)
          ~fov_y:(Float.pi /. 4.) ~width:800. ~height:600. ()

(* camera coordinates: eye at the origin, image plane z = -focal *)
let focal = Camera.focal c in
let p_cam = Pga.point3 0.2 (-0.1) (-2.0) in        (* in front of the eye *)
let ray = Pga.line_through (Pga.point3 0. 0. 0.) p_cam in
let hit = Pga.meet ray (Pga.plane3 0. 0. 1. (-.focal)) in
Nx.to_array (Pga.point_coords hit)
(* [|0.1 *. focal; -0.05 *. focal; -.focal|]: f x / (-z), f y / (-z) *)

Camera.project wraps exactly this: it carries the world point into camera coordinates with Camera.view, builds the ray, and meets it with the image plane. Camera.ray and Camera.image_plane expose the same two objects in world coordinates, which is what you want for drawing the construction — as in the figure above — not for reading image coordinates off.

Orthographic mode keeps the same eye, view motor and image plane but projects by a uniform scale instead of intersecting the ray with the plane. That is the right choice for textbook figures and for exact GA diagrams, which is why the figures in this forest use perspective for scenes and orthographic-style flat drawings for diagrams.

Camera.project_pinhole evaluates the same map in closed form, avoiding the multivector allocations in a per-point loop; the test suite asserts that the two agree. Using the algebra for the definition and the closed form for the hot loop is the pattern of the whole library (Traced algebra: no host branches).
