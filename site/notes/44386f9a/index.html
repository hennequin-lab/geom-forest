The carrier plane of a circle

#ga/model

A circle is a round 2-blade, so it does not contain the point at infinity. But contracting it with still produces a plane:

which is the radical plane of the two spheres and contains the whole circle. That contraction is how you recover the plane the circle lies in, without any coordinate arithmetic.

open Geom
let v3 x y z = Nx.create Nx.float32 [| 3 |] [| x; y; z |] in
let sc x = Nx.scalar Nx.float32 x in

let s1 = Cga.sphere ~center:(v3 0. 0. 0.) ~radius:(sc 1.) in
let s2 = Cga.sphere ~center:(v3 0. 0. 1.) ~radius:(sc 1.) in
let z = Cga.circle_of_spheres s1 s2 in
Nx.to_array (Cga.coords (Cga.circle_center z))   (* [|0.; 0.; 0.5|] *)
Nx.to_array (Cga.circle_normal z)                (* +-z, unit *)
Nx.item [] (Cga.circle_radius2 z)                (* 0.75 *)

Centre and radius follow from the plane and either sphere: the centre is the orthogonal projection of the sphere centre onto the plane, and the radius satisfies , with the distance from the sphere centre to the plane. In the example the centres are one unit apart with equal radii, so the radical plane is the bisector at and the radius is .

This is the model's characteristic mixture: geometrically transparent, algebraically uniform, and requiring one small computation to get a number out (The working representation stores the lower grade).
