The geometric product

#ga/foundation

The geometric product is the Clifford product: associative, distributive, bilinear, and non-commutative. For two vectors it splits cleanly,

into a scalar and a bivector. The scalar part is how much the vectors agree. The bivector part is how much they disagree, and it records the plane. For general elements the product mixes grades: the product of a grade- and a grade- element has grades , so a single product of two multivectors generally touches several grades at once.

open Geom
let pi = 4. *. Float.atan 1. in
let sc x = Nx.scalar Nx.float32 x in
let v3 x y z = Nx.create Nx.float32 [| 3 |] [| x; y; z |] in

let u = E3.vector3 1. 0. 0.
and v = E3.vector3 (1. /. Float.sqrt 2.) (1. /. Float.sqrt 2.) 0. in
E3.to_array (E3.gp u v)   (* [|0.7071; 0.; 0.; 0.7071; 0.; 0.; 0.; 0.|] *)

Two facts make this the workhorse. First, associativity: , so products of many elements are unambiguous and can be evaluated left to right. Second, invertibility is common: an element that is a product of non-null vectors has an inverse, which is what makes versors and hence motions possible.
