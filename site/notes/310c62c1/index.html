The conformal group has ten generators

#ga/model

In Cl(4,1) the versor group is , a double cover of , which is the Möbius group of . Its Lie algebra is spanned by ten bivectors:

Six of them — rotations and translations — preserve and form the Lie algebra of SE(3). That is why a motor is exactly the subalgebra generated by and , and why it needs only eight coefficients (One motor algebra, two models). The dilation scales and ; the transversions do not preserve at all (A transversion bends space without moving infinity).

open Geom

(* the six SE(3) generators, as dense bivectors *)
let rot = [ Cga.op Cga.e2 Cga.e3; Cga.op Cga.e3 Cga.e1;
            Cga.op Cga.e1 Cga.e2 ] in
let tr  = [ Cga.op Cga.e1 Cga.einf; Cga.op Cga.e2 Cga.einf;
            Cga.op Cga.e3 Cga.einf ] in
List.length (rot @ tr)     (* 6 *)

The The commutator makes bivectors a Lie algebra closes on these ten, and the exponential of any of them is available in closed form (Exponentiating a simple bivector closes in three cases). For robotics, the useful habit is to stay in the six: dilations and transversions are occasionally valuable for scale-invariant objectives and for inversion-based constraints, but they are not poses, and they do not belong in a kinematic chain (When the conformal model is worth it).
