In a degenerate algebra the dual becomes the complement

#ga/foundation

The Hodge star needs an invertible The pseudoscalar is the whole space as one blade, which a degenerate signature forbids. What survives is index complement: for each blade , the blade whose index set is the complement of , signed so that

That signed permutation of coefficients is the complement. It is defined for every signature, it is cheap (a gather with precomputed signs), and it is the dual that projective geometry actually uses.

open Geom

let b = Blade.of_int 3 in                      (* e12 *)
Blade.to_string (Blade.complement ~n:3 b)      (* "e3" *)
Blade.complement_sign ~n:3 b                   (* 1: e12 and e3 = e123 *)
Blade.to_string (Blade.complement ~n:4 (Blade.of_basis 1))  (* "e234" *)

Two properties to remember. First, the complement is not the star: sign conventions differ and there is no metric involved beyond the index sets. Second, because it needs no inverse, it composes safely with the regressive product, which is defined through it. In Cl(4,1) the library exposes both, and the numerical note warns about mixing them. In Cl(3,0,1) only the complement exists (PGA has no Hodge dual, and that is the point).
