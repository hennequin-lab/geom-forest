One motor algebra, two models

#ga/motion

The subalgebra of Cl(4,1) generated by is isomorphic to Cl(3,0,1), because the four generators have squares and anticommute. Its even part is the eight-dimensional motor algebra, with playing the role of the degenerate .

So the product, inverse, exponential, logarithm and slerp of motors are the same code in both models. What is not shared is the object layer: a CGA point is a null vector, a PGA point is a trivector, and the embeddings of the motor into 32 or 16 coefficients differ by an automorphism on the dual part (Cl(3,0,1) is plane-based projective geometry).

open Geom

let m = Motor.translate3 1. 0. 0. in
Nx.to_array (Pga.point_coords (Pga.apply_motor m (Pga.point3 0. 0. 0.)))
(* [|1.; 0.; 0.|] *)
Nx.to_array (Cga.coords (Cga.apply_motor m (Cga.point3 0. 0. 0.)))
(* [|1.; 0.; 0.|] *)

The library therefore puts Motor at the top level and gives each model its own of_motor and apply_motor. Reach for the dense version only when you need to compose a motor with something that is not a motor — a dilation, a A transversion bends space without moving infinity, or an arbitrary versor — or when you want the dense The adjoint turns a sandwich into a matrix.

This split is also the answer to "which model should I use?" for pure kinematics: neither. Use Motor (When the conformal model is worth it).
