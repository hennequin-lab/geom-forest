A blade is an ordered product

```typst
e_A = e_(i_1) e_(i_2) dots e_(i_k), quad i_1 < i_2 < dots < i_k,
```

of $k$ distinct basis vectors of an $n$-dimensional algebra. The index set is
$A = {i_1, dots, i_k}$, and $k$ is the grade. Number the basis vectors from
$1$ to $n$ and encode the blade by the integer whose bit $i - 1$ is set exactly
when $e_i$ occurs in it. The empty product (the scalar $1$) has bitmask $0$.
That encoding is the whole data structure, and it gives four rules:

- the [[ad136f1c|grade]] is the population count of the integer;
- the product index is the bitwise exclusive or, because a repeated letter
  cancels: $e_A e_B prop e_(A xor B)$;
- the sign is the parity of the permutation that sorts the concatenation of
  $A$ and $B$;
- the metric factor is the product of $sigma_i$ over the intersection
  $A inter B$ — that is, over the basis vectors that occur in both factors —
  and is zero when a null direction appears in both.

This is the whole multiplication algorithm. For $n = 5$ there are $32$ blades
and $1024$ ordered pairs, so the [[6c390d07|table]] is 4 KiB of
integers, computed once. `Blade` owns the index arithmetic and knows nothing
about the metric, which is why it can be shared by all the models.

```ocaml
open Geom

let b = Blade.of_int 3 in
Blade.to_string b                     (* "e12" *)
Blade.grade b                         (* 2 *)
Blade.mul_sign b b                    (* -1: e12 e12 = -1 *)
Blade.to_string (Blade.complement ~n:3 b)  (* "e3" *)
Blade.complement_sign ~n:3 b          (* 1: e12 and e3 = e123 *)
```

`Blade.all n` returns the blades in increasing bitmask order, which is exactly
the order of the coefficient array of a [[11281e48|multivector]]. The
bitmask encoding is also why the library's storage choice in
[[7d10658e|Store the orthonormal basis, read the null basis]] matters: the xor rule needs orthogonality.
