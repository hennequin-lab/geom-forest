The scalar product is the grade-zero part of the product,
$angle tilde(A) B angle_0$, a single number per pair of multivectors. It is
symmetric in an Euclidean signature, and for vectors it coincides with the
[[f7e286b8|The left contraction is the metric projection]]: $u dot v$. The library exposes it as a function returning a
tensor of the batch shape, because that is usually what you want to compare
against zero.

```ocaml
open Geom

let u = E3.vector3 1. 0. 0.
and v = E3.vector3 (1. /. Float.sqrt 2.) (1. /. Float.sqrt 2.) 0. in
Nx.item [] (E3.scalar_prod u v)   (* 0.7071 *)
```

The reason a library needs both a contraction and a scalar product is that they
differ on mixed grades and on blades in non-Euclidean signatures. The
contraction returns a multivector; the scalar product throws away everything
but the scalar. Incidence tests use the contraction because the *sign* of the
result is a signed distance ([[b24b87b3|Distance is an inner product]]), while normalisation uses
$angle tilde(A) A angle_0$, which is the squared magnitude of a blade
([[e00d2854|Reverse and the magnitude of a blade]], [[fa842ee6|Invert a blade with its reverse]]).

In the conformal model the scalar product of two points is the single most
useful number in the model: $-1/2$ times their squared distance
([[b24b87b3|Distance is an inner product]]). The library's `norm2` is the same function with both
arguments equal.
