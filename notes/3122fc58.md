A Clifford algebra is generated by a real vector space $V$ with a symmetric
bilinear form $g$, subject to one relation:

```typst
u v + v u = 2 g(u, v), quad u, v in V
```

Read it as a specification of how the product fails to commute. The
antisymmetric part of $u v$ is new structure; the symmetric part is the metric
you started with. Choose an orthonormal basis $e_1, dots, e_n$ with
$e_i^2 = sigma_i in {+1, -1, 0}$ and the relation becomes two rules:

```typst
e_i^2 = sigma_i, quad e_i e_j = -e_j e_i quad (i != j)
```

Associativity does the rest. Because the basis vectors anticommute, any word in
them can be sorted, and words with a repeated letter collapse to a scalar. The
survivors are the [[9ae37e30|blades]] $e_A$ over strictly increasing index
sets, of which there are $2^n$. That count *is* the dimension of the algebra,
and it also fixes the storage layout: one coefficient per blade
([[11281e48|A multivector is a coefficient vector over blades]]).

Everything else in this forest is a consequence. [[831bf42e|Perpendicular directions anticommute]] is the
second rule. [[d26e47d0|A signature counts positive, negative and null directions]] is the choice of the $sigma_i$, the one free
parameter of the construction. [[8e33745c|The wedge]] records the
antisymmetric part as an object, and [[b65e7fa9|the product]] keeps
both parts at once.

```ocaml
open Geom

let e1 = E3.e1 and e2 = E3.e2 in
let sq = E3.to_array (E3.gp e1 e1) in            (* 1 on the scalar blade *)
let anti = Nx.add (E3.gp e1 e2) (E3.gp e2 e1) in (* the zero multivector *)
```
