A translation by the vector $bold(t) = t_1 e_1 + t_2 e_2 + t_3 e_3$ is
generated by the bivector $-1 slash 2 bold(t) e_oo$, where $e_oo = e_4 + e_5$
is the point at infinity (a null vector). That bivector is null,
because

```typst
(bold(t) e_oo)^2 = norm(bold(t))^2 e_oo^2 = 0,
```

so the exponential terminates after one term:

```typst
T = exp(-1/2 bold(t) e_oo) = 1 - 1/2 bold(t) e_oo.
```

This is the third case of [[0aa35a84|the bivector exponential]], and it is
why translation is cheap in this formalism: a translation is a *null rotation*,
an even versor with a nilpotent generator, not an affine trick appended from
outside.

```ocaml
open Geom

let t = Motor.translate3 0. 0. 1. in
Motor.to_array t                (* [|1.; 0.; 0.; 0.; 0.; 0.; -0.5; 0.|] *)
Nx.to_array (Cga.coords (Cga.apply_motor t (Cga.point3 0. 0. 0.)))
(* [|0.; 0.; 1.|] *)
```

A translator fixes $e_oo$ as a vector, which is exactly the statement that
translations are Euclidean motions ([[eb4daacf|e∞ is the point at infinity]]). Its sandwich moves
a point by $bold(t)$ and adjusts the $e_oo$ coefficient by exactly the
$1 slash 2 norm(bold(t))^2$ needed to keep the image null — the correction that
an affine matrix does with a homogeneous coordinate and that the conformal
embedding does for free.

Translations compose by addition of their vectors, and they do not commute with
rotations, which is why a general pose needs both parts
([[5b1746c2|Motors compose like matrices]], [[6132d759|The SE(3) exponential turns a twist into a screw]]).
