`Cl(3,0,1)` has three Euclidean directions and one degenerate direction $e_0$
with $e_0^2 = 0$. Its sixteen coefficients are exactly enough for the objects
of three-dimensional projective geometry: a plane is a vector, a line is a
bivector, a point is a trivector. The [[498aa9d8|grade table]] is the whole
object model.

The degenerate direction is what makes it projective. Homogeneous coordinates
identify a point with any non-zero multiple of itself, and $e_0$ carries the
"at infinity" part of that identification: a [[52e66d90|point with zero weight]] is a direction, not a location. The [[afad7126|The pseudoscalar is the whole space as one blade]] squares to zero,
so there is no [[d32870e7|The Hodge dual trades a blade for its complement]]; the dual is the [[b5dd2ed2|In a degenerate algebra the dual becomes the complement]]
([[ccd5c487|PGA has no Hodge dual, and that is the point]]), and the [[35bfa9f1|regressive product]] takes its place.

```ocaml
open Geom

let pi = Pga.plane3 0. 0. 1. 1. in        (* the plane z = 1 *)
let p = Pga.point3 0. 0. 1. in
Nx.item [] (Pga.weight p)                 (* 1.: a finite point *)
Nx.item [] (Pga.is_ideal p)               (* false *)
Nx.item [] (Pga.point_on_plane p pi)      (* true *)
```

Because the basis is already orthogonal ($e_1, e_2, e_3, e_0$ with the last
squared to zero), PGA needs no [[7d10658e|null-basis trick]]. It is
the natural model for graphics and for plane-based incidence, and its
[[a7e0caa1|even part]] is the eight-dimensional
[[146686de|dual-quaternion]] algebra shared with CGA ([[432aee25|One motor algebra, two models]]).
