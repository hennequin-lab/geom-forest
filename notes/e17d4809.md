The logarithm is the inverse of [[6132d759|the exponential]] near the identity.
Given a rotation matrix $R$ and a translation $bold(t)$, with
$theta = arccos((op("tr") R - 1)/2)$ the rotation angle, the twist is

```typst
omega = theta/(2 sin theta) "vee"(R - R^T), quad
bold(v) = V^(-1) bold(t).
```

Here $"vee"$ is the inverse of the hat map: it reads the three independent
entries of a skew-symmetric matrix back as a vector. The matrix $V$ is the left
Jacobian of [[6132d759|The SE(3) exponential turns a twist into a screw]], evaluated at $omega$:

```typst
V = "Id" + (1 - cos theta)/theta^2 Omega
        + (theta - sin theta)/theta^3 Omega^2,
```

with $Omega = hat(omega)$ the skew matrix of $omega$. A rotation by exactly
$pi$ is singular in the naive formula (both $sin theta$ and the skew part
vanish), so the implementation switches to a stable branch there.

```ocaml
open Geom

let m =
  Motor.mul (Motor.translate3 0. 0. 1.)
    (Motor.rotate ~axis:(Nx.create Nx.float32 [|3|] [|0.;0.;1.|])
       ~angle:(Nx.scalar Nx.float32 (Float.pi /. 2.)))
in
Nx.to_array (Motor.log m)   (* the twist, shape [|6|] *)
```

The logarithm is where most robotics code touches Lie theory: a pose error, a
velocity command, and an interpolation are all logarithms or exponentials. In
this forest it is the workhorse behind [[0248bd6b|screw interpolation]] and
[[f159d2a9|inverse kinematics]].

Two cautions. The logarithm is multivalued globally — a rotation by $theta$ and
by $theta + 2 pi$ are the same motor but different twists — so "the" logarithm
always means the branch near the identity. And for a motor with no rotation
part the formula degenerates to the pure translation $bold(v) = bold(t)$, which
the small-angle branch handles. For the dense algebra the same map is
`Versor.log`, currently limited to a single simple bivector ([[0aa35a84|Exponentiating a simple bivector closes in three cases]]).
