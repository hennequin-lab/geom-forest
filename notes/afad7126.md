The pseudoscalar is the top-grade blade, $I = e_1 e_2 dots e_n$: the whole
vector space, oriented. It has one coefficient, and its square is a single
number that characterises the signature:

```typst
I^2 = (-1)^(n(n-1)/2) (-1)^q.
```

In `Cl(3,0,0)`, $I^2 = -1$: the pseudoscalar behaves like the imaginary unit,
which is why the even subalgebra is the quaternions ([[834da58f|Quaternions are the even subalgebra of Cl(3,0,0)]]). In
`Cl(4,1)`, $n = 5$ and $q = 1$, so $I^2 = -1$ again, and $I^(-1) = -I$. In
`Cl(3,0,1)` the square is $0$, because a null direction appears in the product.

```ocaml
open Geom

E3.to_array E3.pseudoscalar                     (* e123 *)
Nx.item [] (E3.scalar_prod E3.pseudoscalar E3.pseudoscalar)  (* -1. *)
Signature.pseudoscalar_square (Signature.projective 3)        (* 0 *)
```

Multiplication by $I$ is the [[d32870e7|Hodge dual]] in a non-degenerate
algebra: it sends a grade-$k$ blade to a grade-$(n-k)$ blade representing the
orthogonal complement. When $I$ is not invertible the same map is still defined
by index complement alone, with precomputed signs, and it is called the
[[b5dd2ed2|In a degenerate algebra the dual becomes the complement]]. That is the difference between the conformal and projective
object layers, and the reason [[ccd5c487|PGA cannot use the star]].
