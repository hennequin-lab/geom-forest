The outer product is the grade-raising part of the
[[b65e7fa9|geometric product]]:

```typst
A and B = sum_(r,s) (A_r B_s)_(r+s).
```

It is associative, bilinear, and antisymmetric on vectors: $u and v = -(v and u)$
and $u and u = 0$. So $u and v$ is not "the area", it is the oriented
parallelogram spanned by $u$ and $v$, with the area as its magnitude. And a
wedge of $k$ independent vectors is a grade-$k$ blade, the subspace they span.
Dependence is visible algebraically: if the vectors are linearly dependent
their wedge is zero.

```ocaml
open Geom

(* coplanar with e12: the wedge vanishes *)
E3.to_array (E3.op (E3.vector3 1. 1. 0.) (E3.op E3.e1 E3.e2))
(* [|0.; 0.; 0.; 0.; 0.; 0.; 0.; 0.|] *)

(* a vector out of the plane spans the whole space *)
E3.to_array (E3.op E3.e3 (E3.op E3.e1 E3.e2))  (* one 1 on e123 *)
```

The wedge is the natural constructor of geometry, because it needs no metric:
it works unchanged in degenerate metrics, where the
[[d32870e7|The Hodge dual trades a blade for its complement]] does not exist. In [[d9428abe|projective geometry]] it *is* the
incidence relation ([[89bf9abd|In PGA, incidence is the wedge]]), and in the conformal model
[[5d4f04c6|objects are built by wedging points]]. The complement of
the wedge is the [[35bfa9f1|regressive product]].
