A local inverse-kinematics step is a Gauss–Newton iteration on a pose error. In
motors, the error is a twist computed by the logarithm
([[e17d4809|The logarithm recovers the twist]]), and the update is applied by the exponential
([[6132d759|The SE(3) exponential turns a twist into a screw]]):

```typst
xi = log(M_"target" M_"current"^(-1)), quad
q <- q + J^+ xi.
```

Here $M_"target"$ is the desired pose, $M_"current"$ the current pose, $xi$ the
six-vector pose error, $q$ the vector of joint coordinates, $J^+$ the
pseudo-inverse of the task Jacobian, and $J^+ xi$ the joint update. The error
$log(M_"target" M_"current"^(-1))$ is expressed in the world frame, because the
left factor $M_"target" M_"current"^(-1)$ acts on the left. For a tool-frame
error, reverse the order: $log(M_"current"^(-1) M_"target")$.

The Jacobian columns are the joint twists expressed in the same frame as the
error, either analytic or obtained by differentiating the chain ([[23f492c8|Forward kinematics is a motor product]]) with
`Rune`.

```ocaml
open Geom

(* the pose error as a twist, in the world frame *)
let error_twist ~target ~current =
  Motor.log (Motor.mul target (Motor.inverse current))   (* shape [|6|] *)

(* move a joint twist into the tool frame *)
let to_tool_frame m xi =
  Motor.log (Motor.mul (Motor.mul m (Motor.exp xi)) (Motor.inverse m))
```

The essential structure is four calls: `Motor.inverse` for the current pose,
`Motor.log` for the error, `Motor.adjoint` to express the error in the right
frame ([[db8d2e5e|The adjoint turns a sandwich into a matrix]]), and `Motor.exp` for the update. Everything else is linear
algebra, and it is ordinary `Nx`, so the same loop runs on CPU or GPU and
batches over several targets at once ([[095f0641|The last axis is the blade axis]]).

The two classic failure modes survive: local minima (the log branch is only
valid near the identity) and joint limits (a constraint on $q$, not on the
algebra). Neither is a defect of the representation; both are why the step is
usually damped.
