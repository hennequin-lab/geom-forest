A multivector is an element of the algebra: a formal sum

```typst
A = sum_(I subset {1, dots, n}) a_I e_I,
```

where $n$ is the dimension of the algebra, the sum runs over the $2^n$ index
sets $I$, $e_I$ is the
[[9ae37e30|basis blade]] of that index set, and $a_I$ is its real
coefficient. In software it is just the array of those $2^n$ coefficients, in
increasing bitmask order. Nothing about the algebra is hidden in the type; the
operations read and write that array.

```ocaml
open Geom

let a = E3.gp E3.e1 E3.e2 in
E3.to_array a                 (* [|0.; 0.; 0.; 1.; 0.; 0.; 0.; 0.|] *)
Blade.to_string (Blade.of_int 3)  (* "e12": index 3 has the coefficient 1 *)
E3.coeff a (Blade.of_int 3)   (* 1. *)
```

`coeff` and `with_coeff` are the accessors that name a blade directly, and
they are the API you want in tests and in debugging. `grade k` keeps one grade
and zeros the rest; `even` and `odd` do it for a whole parity.

The coefficient vector is also the batching axis convention. A single
multivector has shape `[|2^n|]`, a batch of $b$ of them has shape
`[|b; 2^n|]`, and an image of points has shape `[|h; w; 2^n|]`. The leading
axes are ordinary broadcast axes for `Nx`, so every algebra operation is one
batched contraction over the last axis. [[095f0641|The last axis is the blade axis]] spells out the
consequences; [[7d10658e|Store the orthonormal basis, read the null basis]] explains why the coefficient basis is the
orthonormal one even in the conformal model.
