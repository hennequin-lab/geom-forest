For a blade $A$, the product $tilde(A) A$ is a scalar, so inverting is a
division:

```typst
A^(-1) = tilde(A) / (tilde(A) A).
```

Here $tilde(A)$ is the [[e00d2854|Reverse and the magnitude of a blade]] of $A$. A blade with $tilde(A) A = 0$ is
*null* and has no inverse at all; a blade with $tilde(A) A != 0$ is invertible
and is called non-null. Null blades are not pathological in this forest — the
[[ccd5c487|degenerate direction]] $e_0$ is null,
[[50ea1633|conformal points]] are null vectors, and
[[ad74741e|translators]] are generated by a null bivector — they simply
cannot be normalised metrically.

```ocaml
open Geom

let b = E3.op (E3.vector3 2. 0. 0.) (E3.vector3 0. 3. 0.) in
Nx.item [] (E3.norm2 b)                       (* 36. *)
E3.to_array (E3.inverse b)                    (* -e12 / 6 *)
E3.to_array (E3.sandwich b b)                 (* b *)
```

`inverse` implements that formula and does not detect singularity, so
[[1d3a2467|check `norm2` first]] when an object may be null. Because inverting
is a reverse, the [[8a607be0|three involutions]] do most of the sign
bookkeeping in the library. `normalize` is the *metric* normalisation
$A / abs(A)$, where $abs(A) = sqrt(abs(angle tilde(A) A angle_0))$ is the
magnitude defined in [[e00d2854|Reverse and the magnitude of a blade]]; the projective normalisations of [[1656ed74|spheres]],
[[7d7f29a7|planes]] and [[52e66d90|points]] are different operations
with different jobs, and mixing them up is the most common source of silently
wrong geometry.
