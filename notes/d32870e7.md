When the metric is non-degenerate the [[afad7126|The pseudoscalar is the whole space as one blade]] is invertible, and the
right Hodge dual of $A$ is

```typst
A^* = A I^(-1),
```

a blade of grade $n - k$ when $A$ has grade $k$. Geometrically it is the
orthogonal complement: a plane becomes its normal line, a point becomes the
pencil of planes through it. The sign convention matters because the star is
only an involution up to sign. In `Cl(4,1)` we have $I^2 = -1$, hence
$I^(-1) = -I$ and

```typst
(A^*)^* = -A, quad "undual"(A) = A I, quad "undual"(A^*) = A.
```

```ocaml
open Geom

let p = Cga.point3 1. 2. 3. in
let star = Cga.dual p in
Blade.grade (Blade.of_int 15)                 (* 4: the dual of a point *)
Nx.to_array (Cga.coeff (Cga.undual star) (Blade.of_int 1))  (* 1. *)
```

`undual` must be an explicit function, not `dual` twice, precisely because of
that minus sign. And `dual` raises for a degenerate signature, where $I$ has no
inverse: use [[b5dd2ed2|In a degenerate algebra the dual becomes the complement]] there, and never mix the two.

The dual is how the conformal object layer stores the *lower* of two
representatives. [[decc193a|The working representation stores the lower grade]] is the table; [[5d4f04c6|Wedge the points, then dualise]]
is the recipe that produces those duals from wedges of points.
