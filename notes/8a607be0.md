Three sign patterns on grade-$k$ blades do all the bookkeeping:

```typst
tilde(e_A) = (-1)^(k(k-1)/2) e_A, quad
hat(e_A) = (-1)^k e_A, quad
overline(e_A) = (-1)^(k(k+1)/2) e_A.
```

They are [[e00d2854|Reverse and the magnitude of a blade]], grade involution and Clifford conjugation. Each is an
involution, each is determined by the grade, and each has one job.

- $hat(A)$ (grade involution) separates even from odd, so it is the projector
  behind [[a7e0caa1|the even subalgebra]].
- $tilde(A)$ (reverse) is the anti-automorphism that defines the magnitude and
  the inverse ([[fa842ee6|Invert a blade with its reverse]]).
- $overline(A) = tilde(hat(A))$ is the Clifford conjugate, the sign pattern
  $+ - - + + - - dots$. On motors it is the group inverse; on a
  [[834da58f|quaternion]] it is the usual conjugate.

```ocaml
open Geom

let b = E3.op E3.e1 E3.e2 in
Nx.item [] (E3.scalar_prod b (E3.reverse b))          (* 1. *)
Nx.item [] (E3.scalar_prod b (E3.grade_involution b)) (* -1. *)
```

The three compose: conjugation is reverse after grade involution, and any two
of them give the third up to the parity of the grade. The library exposes all
three (`reverse`, `grade_involution`, `conjugate`) plus the
`Blade.*_sign` helpers that the precomputed tables are built from.
