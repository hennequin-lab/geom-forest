The commutator product is half the antisymmetric part of the
[[b65e7fa9|geometric product]]:

```typst
A times B = (A B - B A)/2.
```

On bivectors it is the Lie bracket of the Lie algebra $"so"(p,q)$, and it
measures how two infinitesimal rotations fail to commute. The
[[a7e0caa1|The even subalgebra holds the rotations]]'s bivectors are the Lie algebra of the spin group, so the
commutator is the algebraic source of every structure you would otherwise
import from Lie theory: generators of motion, the adjoint action and the
Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff expansion behind [[6132d759|the exponential]].

```ocaml
open Geom

let b12 = E3.op E3.e1 E3.e2 in
let b23 = E3.op E3.e2 E3.e3 in
E3.to_array (E3.commutator b12 b23)   (* -e13: the third rotation plane *)
```

In the conformal model the six bivectors $e_(i j)$ and $e_i and e_oo$ close
under the commutator into the Lie algebra of `SE(3)`; adding $e_4 e_5$ and the
three $e_i and e_0$ gives the ten-dimensional
[[310c62c1|conformal algebra]]. That is the whole reason a
[[146686de|motor]] can carry a Lie-group structure while looking like a
multivector.

The library exposes it as `Algebra.commutator`, with no claim beyond the
bivector case.
