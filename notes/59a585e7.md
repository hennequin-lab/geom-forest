A versor is a product of invertible vectors, $V = v_1 v_2 dots v_k$. Versors
are the elements that act on the algebra by conjugation, and they are exactly
the elements with an inverse of the form $plus.minus tilde(V)$. After
normalisation, $V tilde(V) = plus.minus 1$, so the
[[fa842ee6|inverse]] is a [[e00d2854|reverse]] and applying a versor costs
two products, not a linear solve.

The versors of a given signature form a group, and its identity component is
the spin group: $"Spin"(3,0,0) tilde.eq "SU"(2)$ double-covers rotations,
$"Spin"(4,1)$ double-covers the Möbius group of $RR^3$
([[310c62c1|The conformal group has ten generators]]). Even versors are [[a7e0caa1|even]], and they are
called rotors when they are Euclidean [[b1f5db79|rotations]], motors when they are
rigid motions ([[146686de|A motor is a dual quaternion]]), translators when they are translations
([[ad74741e|A translator is a null rotation]]).

```ocaml
open Geom

let r = E3.rotor ~axis:(Nx.create Nx.float32 [| 3 |] [| 0.; 0.; 1. |])
                 ~angle:(Nx.scalar Nx.float32 (Float.pi /. 3.)) in
Nx.item [] (E3.scalar_prod r (E3.reverse r))   (* 1.: unit *)
Nx.to_array (E3.to_vector (E3.sandwich r E3.e1))
```

The exponential of a bivector is the standard way to manufacture a versor
([[0aa35a84|Exponentiating a simple bivector closes in three cases]]), and the logarithm inverts it ([[e17d4809|The logarithm recovers the twist]]). `Versor.Make`
is the functor that provides `exp`, `log`, `exp_simple` and `slerp` for any
algebra.
