## The geometric product and a rotor

For vectors $u$ and $v$, the geometric product splits as

```typst
u v = u dot v + u and v,
```

where $u dot v$ is the scalar (dot) product and $u and v$ is the bivector: the
oriented parallelogram they span. A rotor $R$ acts on a vector $x$ by the
sandwich $x |-> R x tilde(R)$. See [[e3|the Euclidean algebra]].

![The geometric product of two vectors is a bivector: the oriented parallelogram they span. A rotor acts on a vector by the sandwich.](figures/e3-product.svg)

## Blades of every grade

Three vectors $u, v, w$ in `Cl(3,0,0)` span a line (grade 1, $u$), a plane
(grade 2, $u and v$) and a volume (grade 3, $u and v and w$). See [[grades]].

![The three grades in the Euclidean algebra: a vector u (grade 1), the parallelogram u and v (grade 2), and the parallelepiped u and v and w (grade 3). Each grade adds one dimension of span.](figures/blade-grades.svg)

## The contraction is a quarter turn

For the orthonormal basis vectors $e_1, e_2$ and the plane $e_12 = e_1 and e_2$,
the left contraction satisfies $e_1 dot e_12 = e_2$ and
$e_2 dot e_12 = -e_1$. See [[contraction]].

![The contraction is a quarter turn in the plane: contracting e1 with the plane e12 gives e2, and contracting e2 with it gives -e1.](figures/contraction-quarter-turn.svg)

## The Hodge dual

In `Cl(3,0,0)` the pseudoscalar is $I = e_123$ and the right dual is
$B^* = B I^(-1)$. For the plane $B = e_12$ this gives the normal vector
$B^* = e_3$. See [[hodge-dual]].

![The Hodge dual in Cl(3,0,0): the plane B = e12 (grade 2) and its orthogonal complement B* = e3 (grade 1).](figures/dual-complement.svg)

## Incidence in PGA

Three planes $pi_1, pi_2, pi_3$ in `Cl(3,0,1)`; the meet of the first two is
their line $l = pi_1 and pi_2$, and the meet of all three is the point
$p = pi_1 and pi_2 and pi_3$. Every incidence is a wedge. See
[[pga-meet-join|meet and join in PGA]].

![Three planes, the line where two of them meet, and the point where the third cuts that line. Every incidence here is a wedge.](figures/pga-incidence.svg)

## Four conformal constructions

With $X_i$ conformal points and $e_oo$ the point at infinity: two points give
the point pair $X_1 and X_2$, three give the circle
$(X_1 and X_2 and X_3)^*$, four give the sphere
$(X_1 and X_2 and X_3 and X_4)^*$, and three points plus $e_oo$ give the plane
$(X_1 and X_2 and X_3 and e_oo)^*$. See
[[objects-from-points|the construction recipe]].

![The four constructions side by side: two points give a point pair, three a circle, four a sphere, and three points plus e-infinity a plane. In each panel the points are the input and the object is the dual of their wedge.](figures/object-construction.svg)

## Wedge the points, then dualise

Three conformal points $X_1, X_2, X_3$ span a triangle; the direct blade
$X_1 and X_2 and X_3$ is their projective span, and its dual $z$ is the
circumcircle. See [[objects-from-points|the construction recipe]].

![Three points on a circle; the circle is the dual of their wedge. The triangle is the direct blade, the circumcircle the constraint.](figures/wedge-dualise.svg)

## Round versus flat

A sphere $S$ and a plane $pi$; the contraction with the point at infinity gives
$e_oo dot S = -1$ for the round object and $e_oo dot pi = 0$ for the flat one.
See [[round-flat]].

![Round versus flat: a sphere has e-infinity dot S = -1, a plane has e-infinity dot pi = 0. The single contraction with the point at infinity decides the family.](figures/round-flat.svg)

## A transversion bends space

A transversion with parameter vector $bold(a)$ is generated by the null
bivector $-a_i e_i and e_0$; it is conformal, so angles survive and only the
scale varies. See [[transversion]].

![A square grid pushed through a transversion: the map is conformal, so right angles survive and only the scale varies.](figures/transversion-grid.svg)

![The eye, a world point, the view ray and the image plane; the projection is the meet of the ray with the plane.](figures/camera-meet.svg)

## The conformal embedding of a line

The coordinate $x$ becomes the conformal point
$X = x + 1 slash 2 x^2 e_oo + e_0$ on a parabola, where $e_0$ and $e_oo$ are
the two null basis vectors of `Cl(4,1)`; the chord $X and Y$ encodes the
distance between the two points. See [[conformal-embedding|the embedding
note]].

![The embedding of a line: the coordinate x becomes a point on a parabola, and the chord length encodes the distance.](figures/null-parabola.svg)

## Three cases of the exponential

A simple bivector $B$ satisfies one of $B^2 < 0$, $B^2 > 0$ or $B^2 = 0$; the
exponential is respectively a rotation, a boost and a translation. See
[[bivector-exp]].

![The three geometries of one plane. A negative square gives a rotation, a positive square a boost (a dilation), and a zero square a translation: the three cases of the bivector exponential.](figures/exp-three-cases.svg)

## One sandwich, every object

A single motor $M$ acts on a point, a sphere, a circle, a line segment and a
plane by $X |-> M X tilde(M)$; dashed shapes are before, solid shapes are
after. See [[sandwich]].

![One sandwich moves every object: a point, a sphere, a circle, a line segment and a plane, all transformed by the same motor. Dashed shapes are before; solid shapes are after.](figures/sandwich-uniform.svg)
